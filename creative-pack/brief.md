# Creative brief: "הקוסם" (The Magician)

Hi team,

We'd like you to make a satirical mobile game about Israeli politics, in Hebrew, for the 2026
election season. Below is what we have in mind. Treat it as direction, not a script: we're coming
to you for your ideas, so push back, surprise us, and make it funnier than we imagined.

## The product
A **mobile-first, portrait, one-thumb idle game** in **full Hebrew**, drawn entirely in **retro
pixel art**. The player is **"The Magician"** (הקוסם), Benjamin Netanyahu's long-running
nickname: he pulls money out of a top hat, pulls coalitions out of thin air, keeps one step ahead
of the courtroom, and calls elections again and again. In this game, as in real life lately, the
elections never really end.

It should feel like a sharp late-night satire sketch you can play on the bus: easy to pick up,
hard to put down, and made to be shared.

## Who it's for
Israeli adults on their phones, across the political map, who follow the news, love a good roast,
and share memes in family and work WhatsApp groups. Most of them don't think of themselves as
gamers. The first ten seconds should make them laugh and teach them the game without a word of
instructions.

## What we want people to say
- "Did you see this? It's hilarious."
- "It roasts *everyone*."
- "I can't stop tapping."

## The core of the game
- **Tap the Magician.** Every tap is a magic trick that pulls shekels out of his hat. Now and then
  the trick goes spectacularly right, and a rabbit hops out. That's our wink at the **"Bugs Bunny
  trial"**; please design your own original rabbit.
- **Money sources:** spend the money on sources that keep it flowing while the phone is in your
  pocket. We imagine:
  - the tired taxpayer (the "sucker");
  - the high-tech worker eyeing relocation;
  - the VAT that keeps rising;
  - the friend who brings cigars and pink champagne;
  - a submarine that surfaces full of commissions;
  - "the Qatari consultants";
  - a bot farm, the "poison machine";
  - at the very top, a golden checkbook from Washington.

  The people paying in are drawn with sympathy; the joke is on where the money goes.
- **The coalition eats money.** Partners need to be paid to stay: Ben Gvir, Smotrich, Deri, the
  ultra-Orthodox party leaders, Yariv Levin, Miri Regev. Each has a signature demand and a
  signature threat to quit (Ben Gvir's, naturally, comes more than once). Stop paying and they
  walk. You need **61** to call the next election, and that's the whole game.
- **The Suitcase** is our running gag: a maroon suitcase with a "DOHA" sticker keeps floating
  across the screen. Catch it for a reward. Miss it, and the news ticker reads "the suitcase
  reached its destination; we didn't know."
- **The courtroom:** shady money builds suspicion. When it boils over, the Magician has to go
  testify and everything slows down. You can always file for a postponement, each time with a new
  excuse. It never really ends, and that's the joke.
- **"Early elections"** is how you level up. Every round resets the board, grows your loyal base
  for good, moves the story forward, and counts up proudly: "election round no. 6, the public is
  thrilled".

## Characters
Big-head pixel caricatures, recognizable from two or three details:
- **Netanyahu:** silver swept-back hair, blue tie.
- **Sara:** platinum-blonde hair, pink blazer.
- **Ben Gvir:** white knitted kippah, glasses.
- **Smotrich:** dark beard, red tie.
- **Deri:** grey beard.
- **Lapid:** silver pompadour, all in black.
- **Bennett:** bald crown, small kippah.
- **Liberman:** bald crown, grey goatee.
- **Eisenkot:** close-shaved grey head, open collar.

Draw them from scratch, never from photos.

**The opposition gets roasted just as hard:**
- Eisenkot leads the polls and made his own campaign game.
- Bennett and Lapid run together (and there's the pledge Bennett signed on TV).
- Gantz and the rotation that never came.
- Liberman won't sit with anyone.
- Golan, plus the new faces of 2026.

Nobody wins this game. That's the point.

**Our narrator is "דובי",** the Magician's spokes-parrot: a parrot in a suit who can only
repeat what he was told ("אין כלום!", "ציד מכשפות!"). He hosts a news ticker packed with short,
absurd headlines that react to what the player does, and he tells the story in little news
flashes after every election round.

## Story and world
The story unfolds one election round at a time:
1. the hat that never runs out;
2. the partners discover the hat;
3. the hat gets subpoenaed;
4. the suitcase turns out to have two addresses;
5. Washington wants a suitcase too.

The finale jokes that the game ends when the elections end, meaning never.

The world moves through four places, each with its own look, mood and music:
1. **the Prime Minister's residence on Balfour Street,** with the protest outside;
2. **the Knesset;**
3. **the courthouse;**
4. **Washington.**

We'd love the scene to fill up visibly as the Magician's "empire" grows, so players see their
progress and not just numbers.

Use them as items, headlines, events or jokes. You pick where they land:

A sourced fact sheet comes with this brief. Anything shown as real must match it.

## Tone
Israeli TV satire energy. Eretz Nehederet is the right reference for **spirit and timing**, but
nothing is copied: every joke is ours. The copy is written **in natural, living Hebrew**, never
translated. Speak to the player in the singular. Deadpan, quick, a little absurd, never cruel.

## Our red lines
- **October 7, the hostages, fallen soldiers and their families are never jokes or game
  elements.** Criticising leaders is fair; victims never are.
- **Never these:** soldiers in uniform, children, real photos, realistic AI faces or voices,
  party logos, state emblems.
- **Never a group as the punchline:** not Arab citizens, not ultra-Orthodox people, not settlers,
  not anyone. We laugh at the politicians and their deals.
- **No invented "facts" presented as real.** Real quotes are exact and marked as quotes. Legal
  matters are described the way the record describes them: "accused", "alleged", "under
  investigation".
- **This is satire, not a campaign:** no "vote for X", no party links, no fake polls or "who's
  leading" numbers.
- **On first launch and on the share page:** a short line saying it's satire, fictional
  caricatures, not affiliated with any party or candidate, and not a factual claim, plus who made
  it and how to reach us.
- **Nothing for sale** with the politicians' faces on it, and no real-world prizes.

## Must-haves for the experience
- It feels native on a phone: portrait, one thumb, big touch targets, readable at arm's length,
  and a nice surprise when you come back hours later ("while you were away, the taxpayers kept
  paying").
- Something new every few minutes: a new character, a scandal headline, a partner's demand, the
  suitcase.
- Juicy feedback everywhere: squashy taps, coin bursts, little celebrations, a buzz in the hand
  where the phone supports it.
- Music and sound with a chiptune feel and a local flavour, all original, easy to mute.
- A link that looks great when shared on WhatsApp, Telegram, X and Facebook (preview image,
  Hebrew title, one-line pitch), a proper icon when added to the home screen, and a funny result
  card to share: "I survived 6 election rounds and 3 court days".
- Reduced-motion and sound options, and numbers that stay readable in the Hebrew layout.

## What we'd like to see from you
1. **A creative pitch:** your take on the concept, anything you'd change, and three alternative
   titles if you have better ones.
2. **The look:** character line-up sketches, the four locations, the title screen and the app
   icon.
3. **The voice:** about 20 sample jokes and headlines in Hebrew, plus Dubi's first story flash.
4. **The playable game,** then the polished, shareable version.
5. **A reference list:** every real quote and affair used, with its source.

Thanks. We can't wait to see what you make of it.
