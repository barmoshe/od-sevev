# Hebrew copy review rubric (reconcile wave)

Every line in `copy-deck.md` and the UX microcopy table is scored against these five gates.
A line that fails any gate gets an objection (per `contracts/objection.md`) carrying a rewritten
line as the `proposed_alternative`. A line that clears all five stays.

1. **Native.** An Israeli reading it aloud would never guess it was drafted in English. Common
   failures:
   - English word order;
   - calqued idioms ("עושה היגיון", "בסוף היום");
   - over-formal register where speech is wanted;
   - "את ה-" chains;
   - translated puns that only work in English.
2. **Funny.** It has a mechanism a reviewer can name:
   - an idiom twist;
   - deadpan news-speak applied to the absurd;
   - an understatement;
   - a rule-of-three turn;
   - a callback;
   - a reversal in the last word.

   "It mentions a scandal" is not a mechanism. The punchline sits at the end of the line.
3. **Short.** Ticker ≤ 45 chars (60 hard cap), flavour ≤ 60, buttons 1-2 words.
4. **True where it claims truth.**
   - A quote is exact and carries a `[src:]`.
   - Legal status matches the record ("נאשם", "חשד", "לפי הפרסומים"). For Qatargate the
     aides are suspected and Netanyahu is not a suspect.
   - Invented news is *plainly* absurd, never plausible-false.
5. **Inside the red lines.**
   - No October 7, hostages, soldiers, reserve duty, sirens, the Iran war or children.
   - No group as the punchline.
   - No "vote X", polls or "who's leading".
   - No private individuals.
   - Nothing copied from Eretz Nehederet.

Balance check across the whole deck: the opposition is roasted roughly as hard as the coalition
(count lines per side; flag if the ratio is worse than 1:2).
